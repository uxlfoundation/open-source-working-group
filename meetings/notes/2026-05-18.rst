Quick Recap
=============

The meeting focused on updates and discussions across multiple oneAPI library projects, including oneTBB, oneMath,
oneDPL, and oneDAL. John and Mike discussed the transition of oneTBB's specification into its documentation, with plans
to eventually integrate it fully into the developer reference. Biagio presented the Linux Foundation education
initiative, proposing to start with four mini-courses for the projects and seeking subject matter experts for each
course. The team reviewed current development activities, with Mike reporting on oneTBB's next release including C++
modules support and NUMA extensions, while Alexey mentioned oneDPL's focus on closing implementation gaps for parallel
differential algorithms and deprecating FPGA support. Maria provided updates on oneMath's plans to update the oneMKL
backend and discussed the potential removal of SYCL buffer interface, which received mixed feedback from the
team. Biagio concluded by sharing discussions from the SYCL Working Group regarding the deprecation of buffer interface
and specialization constants, noting that buffer performance is generally lower than USM and that the buffer interface
has many corner cases that need handling.

oneMath Specification Discussion Meeting:
===========================================

John and Biagio discussed the oneMath specification while waiting for other participants to join the meeting. John
mentioned that he had joined a Math SIG the previous week to discuss this specification, noting that it faced similar
challenges as oneTBB and oneDPL.

Updated Specification Implementation Plans:
=============================================

Mike announced that they published an updated specification on GitHub, eliminating version numbers and embracing a
dynamic specification approach. The team discussed plans to integrate the specification into their documentation over
time, with most API content moving to the reference section while preview features remain in the reference section. John
noted that oneMath needs to implement a similar approach, and they discussed updating website links to point to the new
integrated specification and documentation structure.

oneMath Documentation and Video Plans:
========================================

The team discussed moving oneMath specifications back into their documentation, with John explaining that while other
components like oneDNN and oneDAL had dropped specification maintenance, oneMath still needs its architectural content
which isn't duplicated elsewhere. John clarified that Nick from ARM is now the lead maintainer of oneMath. The meeting
then shifted to plans for creating explainer videos for each library, with John outlining a process where he would
collect current slide decks and marketing materials from projects, create initial video drafts and transcripts, then
return them to projects for review and approval before recording and publishing the final videos on the new website.

Linux Foundation Educational Platform Planning:  (`LF Education Slides`_)
===========================================================================

The team discussed implementing a Linux Foundation educational platform starting with four mini-courses, with Biagio
explaining that the initial phase would involve free content with no budget required from the project. The plan involves
identifying subject matter experts (SMEs) for each course, with Mike Walsh suggested as a potential SME, and the content
would start as free microlearning videos before potentially expanding to paid certifications in the future. The team
agreed to start with mini-courses on 6 projects and decided to provide feedback to Kara about the course strategy at the
next open source meeting.

Library Projects Development Updates:
=======================================

The team provided updates on their respective library projects. Mike reported that oneTBB is working on its next release
with code freeze approaching, including support for C++ modules and NUMA extensions, with plans for four releases per
year featuring major releases in spring and fall. Alexey discussed oneDPL's progress on closing implementation for
parallel differential algorithms accepted for C++26, with plans to propose a cleaner version through the RFC process
within the next year, including deprecating FPGA support. Maria shared that the oneMKL backend team plans to update
oneMKL following a recent public release and is discussing making the SYCL buffer optional, seeking feedback on known
use cases for this feature.

SYCL Buffer Accessory Discussio: (`SYCL Slides`_)
===================================================

The team discussed potential changes to SYCL, including the removal of buffer accessory in favor of USM, which would
reduce binary size and address performance issues. Biagio presented feedback from the SYCL Working Group regarding
several proposed changes, including buffer accessory deprecation, adaptive CPP support, and specialization
constants. The group expressed mixed opinions about deprecating buffer accessory, with some noting implementation
challenges and performance concerns, while others supported keeping it as an optional feature.

The conversation ended with a decision to move to quarterly meetings going forward, with special meetings as needed
based on feedback from participants.
